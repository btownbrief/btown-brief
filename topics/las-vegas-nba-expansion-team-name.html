NBA Expansion

Las Vegas NBA Team Name Speculation

As fans imagine a Las Vegas NBA franchise, the debate over what to call it taps into a league that has been expanding and rebranding since its earliest days.

Read

A League Built on Growth

The NBA has never been static. It began with 11 teams in 1946 and has expanded repeatedly since to its current 30-team footprint.

The most recent wave of new franchises came in stages: the Charlotte Hornets and Miami Heat joined in 1988, followed by the Minnesota Timberwolves and Orlando Magic in 1989, then the Toronto Raptors and Vancouver Grizzlies, and later the New Orleans Hornets. Each addition required the league to settle on a name and identity before a single game was played.

How New Teams Get Built

When Toronto and Vancouver joined the league, the process didn't stop at naming. The 1995 NBA expansion draft, held on June 24, 1995, let the two new franchises stock their rosters for the 1995–96 season. Toronto had been awarded its franchise on September 30, 1993, and Vancouver on April 27, 1994 — both identities settled well before either team took the floor.

Those two clubs were also the first NBA teams in Canada since the 1946–47 Toronto Huskies. Expansion revives old questions of identity, and Las Vegas, with no NBA history to draw on, would be starting from scratch.

The Name Game, Unofficially

No Las Vegas NBA franchise has been named, but that hasn't stopped fans from filling the void. A widely shared prompt on r/NBA — asking, without trying to be a comedian, what a good name for the team would be — captures the tone: half genuine brainstorm, half running joke.

That speculation has spilled into video too, with power-ranking rundowns and reaction clips treating the naming question as its own mini-sport.

The 1995 expansion draft — which turned the newly named Raptors and Grizzlies into functioning rosters — is the template Las Vegas would likely follow.
