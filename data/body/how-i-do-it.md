# How I do it

I started this list for myself. Every day I'd tell myself I was going to work out more, drink more water, read more, and every day some of it slipped. So I wrote down what I actually want to do daily, and a few things I want to do a few times a week, and I built a place to check them off. This page is that list, and the reasons behind it. Copy the whole thing into your Today tab, or take the parts that fit your life and leave the rest.

## The list

Some of these are daily and some are a few times a week. That's on purpose. Flossing three times a week that actually happens beats a daily goal I quit after ten days.

- **Brush my teeth** every day, and **floss** a few times a week, at least once.
- **DermaDry** for my hands and feet several times a week. It's a treatment where you submerge your hands and feet, and it keeps me from sweating through everything as much as I do.
- **Acne medication** every day, so my face stays clear and hydrated.
- **Breathing breaks** through the day. More on that below.
- **The gym** three times a week, and **sprints** at least once.
- **Water**, a **protein shake** and **creatine** every day.
- **Log my calories** with a photo.
- **Track my sleep.**
- **Read** a little every day.
- **Record a voice note** whenever I have an idea.
- **Plan my day** with Claude and Grok Bot, which catch my email and what's coming up so I start the morning knowing the plan.

## Working out is easier than you think

Working out is super easy when you break it down. If you're a beginner, all you really need is the machines. A lot of people think they have to do free weights to get results, and I'm pretty sure machines will get you just as far. Find a gym with good machines, whether that's Crunch, Planet Fitness or the Edge, which is really nice, and start there. The Gyms tab has every gym around Burlington with what it has and what a day pass costs.

What matters is effort and knowing how each machine works. Set the seat so the handles line up with you, find the little dot where the machine pivots and line it up with your joint, and pick a weight where the last couple of reps are hard. Then write it down. I keep a little log of the machine, the settings, the weight, the reps and how many more I could have done. Next time I read the line and go, and when the reps get easy, I add weight. That's the whole system.

There are three routines in the Train tab, a beginner one, an intermediate one and a hardcore one, all on machines, plus a sprint session. Sprints are really easy to fit in. Warm up, run hard for about twelve seconds, walk back, do it again a few times, and you're done in twenty minutes.

## Food matters as much as the workout

Diet is as important as working out, especially if you're trying to lose weight, and for a lot of people weight is the thing that makes the biggest difference in how they look and feel. Two basics carry most of it. Protein, enough that every meal has some and a shake fills in the rest, and creatine, five grams a day, every day. The Basics tab has the numbers and where they come from.

Calories are the other half. I'm starting to log mine, and the trick is making it so easy you don't skip it. MacroFactor lets you take a photo of your plate and it estimates what's on it, so logging a meal takes about ten seconds. You can fix anything it gets wrong, and over a couple of weeks you learn what your normal day actually looks like.

## Recovery is almost as important

Rest and recovery are almost as important as the workout, and I want them to be a daily thing too. Guys don't usually do yoga, but most of us don't mind stretching on a mat or foam rolling for ten minutes. The Recover tab has a ten-minute stretch, a twenty-minute yoga routine for people who don't do yoga, and a foam rolling routine, each with a timer. It also lists where to sauna, cold plunge or get a massage around town, with prices.

Sleep is the big one. It's when all the work pays off. I track mine, and the Sleep tab compares the trackers that actually work, the Apple Watch, Fitbit, Oura, WHOOP, or just your phone, with what each one measures and costs.

## Breathing through the day

I try to take small moments through the day to calm down. My go-to is a breathing exercise I picked up from Andrew Huberman, where you breathe in through your nose, take a second little breath on top of it, and then let it all out slowly. Doing that for a couple of minutes really settles me. It's become more important since I've been getting into caffeine, and the Breathe tab has a timer so you can just follow along.

## Water

Such an easy thing to do, and such an easy thing to forget. I want to hit a water goal every day and see how I've been doing over the past few weeks, so the Fuel tab has a simple tap counter that remembers.

## Reading and voice notes

I want to read more, so it's on the list every day, even if it's a few pages. And I record voice notes constantly. Whenever I have an idea I hit a shortcut on my phone, start talking, and the transcript does the rest. The Coach tab shows how to set that up on an iPhone.

## Do it with other people

Everything here is easier with company. The Together tab shows what other people are doing this week, workouts and recovery both, so you can just show up and join. I'm also turning the Adult Social Sports group on Meetup, almost 2,000 people, into a group for working out and recovering together, with events by age so everyone feels like they belong.

That's how I do it. Copy the list, make it yours, and if you want company at the gym, come find me.
