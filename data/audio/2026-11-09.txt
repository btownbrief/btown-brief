[pause] Beta announces its new training center even as South Burlington ponders plan.

"It’s really how Beta is going to be leaning into training pilots, training aircraft mechanics and really growing that programming in a meaningful way," Gagnon said, per The Other Paper.

Beta Technologies has filed its first building project under its proposed master plan, a 61,000 square foot addition at 430 Davinci Drive that would create a four story training center and an adjoining aircraft hangar. The plans include six of Beta's full scale "ThunderDome" flight simulators, and Beta's Alex Gagnon says all the pilots for the company's electric fleet will train there. The project depends on the master plan, which South Burlington is still weighing with a decision due by Nov. 20, and Beta returns to the Development Review Board with revisions Nov. 17. If it's approved, Beta plans to finish in 2028, after a traffic light goes in at Williston Road and Davinci Drive.

[pause] New Burlington police chief will face tall order.

"We have half a police department right now," Bouchett said, per VTDigger.

The City Council is expected to vote Monday, Nov. 9, on Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak's pick for chief, Anthony Geraci, a 25 year law enforcement veteran who has served as police chief in Watervliet and Newburgh, New York. If the council approves him, he starts Tuesday, with interim chief Shawn Burke staying until March to help with the transition. He would take over a department down to 54 officers with 19 vacancies. Hiring committee member Mark Bouchett of the Church Street Marketplace commission and Lake Champlain Chamber CEO Catherine Davis want recruiting to be a priority, while residents including former police commissioner Stephanie Seguino want a chief open to civilian oversight.

[pause] Burlington City Council president launches bid for mayor.

"Burlington is stuck at the moment," Traverse said, per VTDigger.

City Council President Ben Traverse, a Democrat who first won the South End's Ward 5 seat in 2022, launched his campaign for mayor Friday at the Battery Park pavilion, setting up a possible race against Progressive Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak, who says she'll announce her reelection plans in the next few days. [pause] Despite $928 Million Spent, Lake Champlain Cleanup Is Falling Behind.

"Halfway through the two-decade timetable, Vermont has achieved only 35 percent of the goal, according to the state’s most recent estimates," per Seven Days.

A Seven Days and ProPublica investigation finds Vermont's Lake Champlain cleanup faltering more than a decade after the 2015 Clean Water Act was signed on the Burlington waterfront, and the EPA warned last year the state would need to more than double its pace to hit the 2036 target. Seven of the lake's 15 sampling stations have topped EPA phosphorus limits every year since 2015, with agriculture the largest polluting sector, as state agencies have repeatedly delayed tougher action on farms, businesses and housing developments. Agency of Natural Resources Secretary Julie Moore says the projects are on pace even though the lake data is slower to show it. A companion story found the outside audit lawmakers mandated for the $928 million spent since 2016 never happened because no firms bid, and Sen. Anne Watson says she'll likely discuss reviving it with colleagues when the session starts in January.

[pause] More Vermonters searching for meals as food shelves try to keep up.

"We are now seeing about 100 more families each month than we did at this time last year," Ginger Morton of the Williston Food Shelf said, per WCAX.

The Vermont Foodbank says its partner network has seen a 30% increase in guest visits compared to 2025, which the Foodbank's Carrie Stahler ties to rising costs and new 3SquaresVT eligibility restrictions that left more than 5,300 Vermonters without benefits between July 2025 and 2026. The Williston Food Shelf now spends $14,000 a month on food, up from $10,000 last year. On the brighter side, students and volunteers carried frozen turkeys from Patrick Gymnasium to Virtue Field Saturday for UVM's 13th Trotting of the Turkeys, which organizers said was over $18,000 and hoping to reach $20,000 for Feeding Champlain Valley.

[pause] Private Equity Firms Are Quietly Buying Up Local Funeral Homes.

"Most of the time, the public never notices the change of ownership because the name on the building and website remains the same," per Seven Days.

Private equity only moved into Vermont's death care industry in the past five years, and Milestone Funeral Partners of Auburn, Maine, now operates 119 funeral homes, crematories and cemeteries across New England and upstate New York, 12 in Vermont, and it has since bought the group of funeral homes that LaVigne Funeral Home in Winooski owned. CEO Michael Martel says consolidating operations lets Milestone offer perks most independents can't, like paid internships, tuition reimbursement and health insurance. Critics say prices climb, and Randolph funeral director Rachel Bishop heard from colleagues that cremation in the Barre-Montpelier area went from $1,995 to $3,500 in two years. Tom Harty of the Vermont Funeral Directors Association says he doesn't know a single person who switched to Milestone who isn't working harder and longer for less money.

[pause] South Burlington School District settles with teachers over labor dispute.

"The unfair labor practice charges, however, never made it to the Vermont Labor Relations Board. Instead, the district and union settled the issues through their own internal negotiations over the past year," per The Other Paper.

The South Burlington Educators Association and Vermont NEA filed the charges in August 2025 after the district hired former Attorney General William Sorrell to investigate a complaint about a "black book" allegedly circulated at teachers' social gatherings with negative commentary about district leadership, an investigation the union called retaliation. Under the settlement the union withdrew its charges and grievance, and the district agreed the activity in the complaint, which the union denies, would be legally permitted concerted activity even if true, and that it will decide whether union activity is protected before investigating future complaints. The district's lawyer denied The Other Paper's request for the Sorrell report in January, citing ongoing litigation, and the paper has requested it again since the settlement.

[pause] Feds Target Vermont Protesters Under New Rule.

"My understanding, from colleagues around the country, is this theory has never seen the light of day," Appel said, per Seven Days.

Peter Booth of Jericho, Henry Prensky of Burlington and Sherri Wormser of Colchester, cited at sit-ins outside the ICE intelligence center on Industrial Avenue, pleaded not guilty Thursday to a federal petty misdemeanor that carries up to 30 days in prison. It's the first time Vermont protesters arrested there have faced federal charges, under a Homeland Security rule that took effect in November 2025 without the usual public comment period, and their attorney Robert Appel plans to file a motion to dismiss. Since then, almost 300 people marched in South Burlington from the White Cap Industrial Park to the Harvest Lane facility, and some at the peaceful sit-in were handed a paper saying they're under Department of Justice investigation for obstructing access to federal property.

[pause] 2 Green Mountain Transit buses sit idle after manufacturer declares bankruptcy.

"It just got to the point where some of the issues that they were having were beyond our capacity to repair," Clark said, per WCAX.

Green Mountain Transit got two Proterra electric buses through a federally funded program in 2020, but problems started soon after, at one point leaving the agency unable to open their doors, and Proterra's 2023 bankruptcy ended parts and maintenance support. GMT is keeping them in storage at little to no cost because getting rid of them would mean paying back the federal grant, and General Manager Clayton Clark doesn't expect them back on the road. Its other electric buses, which Proterra didn't make, work well.

[pause] Quick hits.

Crews start work on I-89 walk-bike bridge in South Burlington — Construction began in late July on the roughly $27 million South Burlington Walk-Bike Bridge over I-89, just south of the Williston Road overpass at Exit 14, and this phase will connect Williston Road and the University Mall. When it's done in 2028, the city will maintain it year round, with nighttime lighting among its safety features.

Betsy Dumont selected as UVM’s next provost — UVM picked Elizabeth "Betsy" Dumont, executive vice chancellor and provost at UC Merced, as its next provost and senior vice president, succeeding interim provost Linda Schadler. The trustees' executive committee is expected to approve her Nov. 16, and she'd begin on campus Jan. 4.

Burlington proposal would shield voter citizenship status — A proposed charter change would keep voters' citizenship status and personal details like birth dates and phone numbers out of public records, building on Burlington's 2023 change letting legal residents who aren't U.S. citizens vote in city elections. After hearings planned for Jan. 13 and 19, voters would decide on Town Meeting Day, March 2, and the Legislature would then need to approve it.

Survey: Half of Vermont businesses hiked prices this year — A Vermont Chamber of Commerce survey of 135 businesses found half raised prices this year, a third delayed hiring or left positions unfilled, and just 10% expanded or invested in Vermont. Healthcare topped the list of pain points, and more than 40% rated the state's business climate poor or very poor.

Vermont and Nova Scotia sign 3-year clean energy agreement — Gov. Phil Scott and Nova Scotia Premier Tim Houston signed a three year agreement to collaborate on offshore wind, transmission planning and grid reliability, aiming to bring Nova Scotia wind power to New England to help hold down costs.

Vermont to receive $4.5M settlement from General Motors following data privacy investigation — Vermont's share of a $220 million nationwide settlement is $4.5 million, after an investigation by 35 states and D.C. that Vermont helped start into GM sharing drivers' data, like speeds and seat belt use, with insurers. GM doesn't admit to breaking any law, says it ended its Smart Driver program in 2024, and the payout goes to the state's General Fund.

Border patrol provides airplane crew as search for missing boater on Lake Champlain continues — As of NBC5's Monday morning update, crews were still searching Kingsland Bay in Ferrisburgh for a 48 year old Burlington man who fell from a small fishing boat around 4 p.m. Saturday and was not wearing a life jacket. The Coast Guard and Vermont State Police expected to resume Monday morning with help from a U.S. Border Patrol airplane crew, and police don't believe the disappearance is suspicious.

Shelburne fires police officer who made pit stop with prisoner — Shelburne fired Officer Matthew Collins on Sept. 8 after an internal investigation found he left a handcuffed prisoner alone in a locked cruiser outside a St. Albans convenience store for about eight minutes during a bathroom stop and turned off his cameras, though Collins says the dismissal was extreme. Officer Daniel Delgado, a former Burlington police lieutenant, has since resigned, leaving the department down three patrol officers.
