This is the Btown Brief for Friday, October 30th (Halloween Weekend).

Keep an extra layer near the costume this weekend, because Halloween looks dry, sunny and chilly. Friday stays cooler and breezy with more clouds than sun, a high in the mid 50s and gusts to about 25 from midday through tonight, and while a stray shower is possible, most places stay dry. Saturday is sunny and tops out around 50, and trick or treat runs from the upper 40s at 4 to about 40 by 9 with clear skies and barely any wind. Clocks fall back an hour Saturday night, and Sunday is the nicest day of the weekend, sunny and about 57 with the evening in the upper 40s. Monday reaches about 60 with more clouds and stays dry through the day. Burlington Right Now keeps the forecast going all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores.

[pause] Charlie’s Boathouse to turn into public park after family sells to Winooski Valley Park District.

"It's literally at the river mouth, so it's highly unique, and it is kind of a puzzle piece," Warner said, per the Colchester Sun.

The boat rental and snack bar spot on the Burlington bike path by the Winooski River bridge, which ran until Charlie died in 2021, is becoming a public park after his family sold the structure and land to the Winooski Valley Park District. Director Nick Warner says the site has 600 feet of shoreline and a conservation easement, so the plan leans toward non motorized recreation, with visitors arriving by the bike path and a new ramp as the first phase. The public can weigh in on the site and the historic building through WVPD's website and hearings in the coming weeks, and it's likely to open sometime in the spring.

[pause] Perkins Museum of Geology reopens at UVM after six-year closure.

"And it's also very entertaining. People, when they come here, are clearly struck by the awesomeness of some of the exhibits," per NBC5.

Closed since 2020, first for COVID and then for staffing shortages, UVM's geology museum is open again with the Charlotte Whale, Vermont's official state fossil, among its displays and student interns helping develop and present the exhibits. It's free and open Monday through Friday from 9 to 4, with a new exhibit on the global diversity of the collection planned for spring.

[pause] Phil Scott built a broad coalition. Now it's showing cracks.

"I think in this race we see a wave, a blue wave, coming across the country that is even affecting Vermont," he said, per Vermont Public.

Gov. Phil Scott won 71% of the vote two years ago, and Vermont Public reports that coalition is fracturing as he asks for a sixth term that would make him Vermont's longest serving governor. The latest poll from State Navigate has Scott at 49 and Democratic/Progressive challenger Amanda Janoo at 46, with a margin of error of 2.3 points. At Wednesday's WCAX debate, Janoo pressed her Fair Share proposal to tax income over $250,000 and help stand up universal primary care, while conceding she doesn't know how the budget gets to a full universal system, which she called the "ultimate destination." Scott answered that "the math has to add up." Scott's signature issue remains his opposition to new taxes, and the Republican Governors Association has spent more than $930,000 through a pro Scott super PAC on ads attacking Janoo. Election Day is Tuesday, Nov. 3.

[pause] Burlington Mayor Taps Anthony Geraci as City’s Next Police Chief.

"I am very confident in advancing his appointment as our next chief of police," she said, per Seven Days.

Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak's pick began with the NYPD in 1998, spent about 15 years with Albany police, and later ran the Watervliet and Newburgh departments in New York. He left Newburgh in 2024 after three women accused him of gender discrimination, retaliation and creating a hostile work environment. He was cleared of any official wrongdoing and called the claims a smear campaign, and the mayor says her administration dug into the complaint and she checked his references herself. The council will be asked to approve him on Nov. 9, with the department down to 55 officers as of August from 92 in July 2020.

[pause] FBI investigates former Burlington school administrator in alleged $1.8 million fraud.

"If these allegations are proven true, our district and community are victims, and we will seek to recover all funds and bring them back to our students," Flanagan wrote, per VTDigger.

Federal agents searched the Colchester home of Stephanie Phillips on Oct. 16, and an affidavit says they believe the longtime district administrator, who retired in May as director of teaching and learning, used a consulting company to obtain at least $1,769,000 from the district since March 2013. Investigators allege she billed through the company for her regular work and for work that was never done. Phillips has not been charged and did not immediately respond to VTDigger, and the district says it has tightened its invoicing procedures and spending limits.

[pause] Burlington Councilors Seek to Compromise on Public Safety Debate.

"If I'm listening to the best angels on everybody's shoulders, I see the possibility of a fruitful conversation," said Councilor Gene Bergman (P-Ward 2), who proposed the committee, per Seven Days.

Shortly after midnight at Monday's meeting, councilors voted 9-3 to form an ad hoc committee of Bergman, Allie Schachter, Council President Ben Traverse and Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak to work out a compromise on Burlington Forward. The Democrats' resolution would put more police downtown, increase drug law enforcement, bar new needle exchanges and a planned overdose prevention site from opening downtown, and create sanctioned campsites, and the mayor has promised to oppose any resolution that prohibits the needle exchanges or the overdose prevention site downtown. The committee reports back to the full council on Dec. 7, with no guarantee of a finished deal.

[pause] Crews work to restore clock faces on iconic Burlington church.

"You can't go to Walmart and buy this part. We have to make it," per WCAX.

Hilltown Clock Repair and Clockworks Inc., both out of western Massachusetts, are restoring the faces and mechanisms on the First Unitarian Universalist tower at the top of Church Street, with a new hand going on each face. The congregants who hand crank and wind it every five to seven days have been out of that job for the past couple years. The congregation will celebrate on Sunday, Nov. 15, at 11:45 AM, right before the clock chimes at noon.

[pause] Supporters raise offer for Rice Memorial High in hopes of spurring court approval of sale.

"The diocese has discussed the purchaser's offer with the official committee," church lawyers have written in court papers, per VTDigger.

Supporters of the 400 student South Burlington campus raised their bid from $4.3 million to $5.9 million, after creditors representing more than 100 people with clergy abuse claims contested the first offer. A market estimate put the 42 classroom building and 27 acres at $4 million to $6 million, while a church commissioned appraisal said $30.5 million. Church lawyers now say the creditors' committee consents, and a judge still has to sign off, with a hearing scheduled for Nov. 20.

[pause] Vermont Pub & Brewery to Close After 38 Years.

"It's hard to tell the story of Vermont craft beer without starting with Vermont Pub & Brewery," Vermont Brewers Association executive director Emma Arian said, per Seven Days.

The brewpub at 144 College Street closes at the end of service on Nov. 14, just days after its 38th anniversary, with the owners saying business never got back to where it needed to be after 2020. Greg and Nancy Noonan opened it in fall 1988, when there were only 11 brewpubs in the United States, after Greg Noonan and the late Bill Mares drafted the law that made them legal in Vermont. There's still time for a last Burly Irish Ale, with beer and food specials in its final days.
