[pause] Vermont celebrates its first Vermont Libraries Day.

"Every Vermonter has a library, and every library has something to offer the community it serves," per Vermont Business Magazine.

Today is the first Vermont Libraries Day, a new annual statewide observance the Legislature created this spring as Act 127. State Librarian Catherine Delneo says the best way to mark it is to go, whether that means signing up for a library card, checking out a book, taking part in a program or borrowing a museum or state park pass. Public libraries are free to everyone, and many let you check out gear for gardening, sports, home repair or cooking, plus science and nature kits for families.

[pause] Vermont will restore fire towers on Spruce and Elmore mountains using helicopters this fall.

"I think people are going to feel a bit more secure, especially folks that are on the bell curve of physicality, age," per Vermont Public.

The state is replacing the steep and sometimes rickety wooden staircases on the Elmore and Spruce fire towers with metal steps and railings like the ones at Allis State Park, and because both summits are so high and remote, a helicopter will carry up roughly 3,000 pounds of materials. The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Elmore tower in 1939, and Spruce's steel tower was moved there from St. Albans in 1943, and each restoration costs almost $250,000. Both towers are expected to stay closed through the end of October, though hikers can still reach the Spruce Mountain vista except during helicopter landings.

[pause] OnLogic to Be Acquired for $675 Million by Omron, a Japanese Firm.

"Crucially for our local community, our global headquarters, with all business functions including manufacturing and engineering operations, will remain right here in South Burlington," per Seven Days.

Omron, a Kyoto automation company with 26,000 employees, is buying the South Burlington computer hardware maker that Roland and Lisa Groeneveld started at their kitchen table in 2003 as Logic Supply, and the deal is expected to close in January. OnLogic will keep its leadership, brand and local workforce, which includes 160 people in Vermont, and every employee gets a thank you bonus of $1,500 for each month they've worked there, which comes to $90,000 for a five year employee. The company builds hardware that can survive tough environments for clients that have included NASA, Amazon and General Motors.

[pause] Vermont’s ‘diverging diamond’ receives positive reviews.

"I’m generally finding that coming out of Costco is a lot easier and getting on Interstate 89 at the end of the day is a lot easier, so I think in general it’s been very positive," per WCAX.

A month after Vermont's first diverging diamond opened in Colchester on Sept. 17, the overall message from drivers is positive, though some say the new signals have made their commute worse and the cones are still out. Crews are installing curbing, sidewalks and permanent traffic signals before winter, VTrans may adjust the light cycles soon, and the hope is to wrap up by mid November. Paving on the far north and south ends along Routes 2 and 7 comes next spring.

[pause] The Burlington-Area Mexican Food Scene Grows in Unexpected Places.

"But we want to share our culture, and we don’t want to lose our traditions here," per Seven Days.

Seven Days found two of the area's best new Mexican spots in unlikely places. Doña Esa, Ada Martínez's family business, now serves dine in dinner Tuesday through Saturday at Doma Bar on Pine Street, with homestyle regional dishes like guisados, chiles en nogada and a mole rosa that gets its pink from beets and hibiscus, and Doma is displaying a Día de los Muertos altar from mid October through early November. In the University Mall food court, Taquería Don Richard has built a big TikTok following on tacos made with handmade tortillas. The combo plates are the best deal on the menu.

[pause] Vermont cannabis event permits could bring legal sales to community events.

"To be able to set up at a festival or a wedding venue would be huge to getting our brand out there," per WCAX.

A pilot program would let licensed cannabis retailers sell from a sanctioned area at events like concerts, farmers markets and sporting events, though nobody could consume on site. Growers and shops say it would help in a market made up of some 300 small cultivators, where a storefront is often too expensive and strict advertising rules make it hard to get the word out. The Cannabis Control Board is expected to give final approval at its meeting this Friday, Oct. 23.

[pause] Man Claims Winooski Police Fractured His Neck, Ankle, Hand and Fingers During Arrest.

"The lawsuit estimates that the two cops are about 6 feet tall, with Willingham weighing well over 200 pounds. White, meanwhile, stands 5 feet 6 inches tall and weighs 110 pounds," per Seven Days.

A lawsuit filed this month in Chittenden Superior Court says two Winooski officers broke Marc White's neck, ankle, hand and two fingers while taking him down in his apartment building's vestibule in February 2025, then told EMTs the blood on his face came from a fall on the sidewalk. White has paranoid schizophrenia that was well known to the department, and the suit says the officers chased him over a call that turned out to involve a thrown plastic cup, the opposite of what the state's use of force policy asks for with people in mental health crisis. Winooski's city manager and police chief declined to comment.

[pause] Engaged young voters across Vermont aim to get their peers to the polls.

"My peers, they see what’s happening and they want to create change, and they really do care," per VTDigger.

Students at UVM and Middlebury are running voter registration drives ahead of the Nov. 3 election, with UVM's helping classmates request absentee ballots, and UVM's nonpartisan Political Science Club says attendance is picking up again after falling off following 2024. Young voters usually turn out the least of any age group, 25.7% in Vermont in the 2022 midterms, but a national CIRCLE survey this year found 56% of young people say they're extremely likely to vote. Phil Scott has been governor since this year's young voters were 8 to 19, and some, like UVM student government's Cameron Laychak of Milton, say they're voting for Amanda Janoo this time.

[pause] Vermonters Will Vote on Two Constitutional Amendments This Fall.

"The storm is coming, but we’re not pulling out our umbrellas fast enough," per Seven Days.

On Nov. 3, Vermonters get the final say on two constitutional amendments, confusingly numbered Proposal 3 and Proposal 4, after two rounds of lawmakers signed off. Proposal 3 would guarantee workers the right to join a union and block any future right to work law, which Rep. Conor Casey calls largely a defensive measure, while the Right for Vermont Foundation argues it takes away union shop workers' freedom to decline paying dues. Proposal 4 guarantees equal protection under the law regardless of race, ethnicity, sex, religion, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression and national origin. A recent UNH poll found about two thirds of likely voters support both.

QUICK HITS

Busy Burlington road to close for 3 days — Main Street closes between South Prospect and South Willard from Tuesday at 7 a.m. until about 5 p.m. Friday for the Summit Street water main project, with a detour through College or Maple streets and no parking. Northbound traffic can still use Summit Street but can't turn onto Main.

Breeze Airways adds new routes from Burlington airport — Breeze is adding flights from Burlington to Myrtle Beach, with a BreezeThru on to Fort Lauderdale without getting off the plane, starting at the end of May next year. The airport says 50 to 70 local passengers a day want that Myrtle Beach flight, and it's teasing a new warm weather international destination to be announced before the end of October.

UVM expands tuition-free program for Vermonters — Starting in fall 2027, UVM Promise covers tuition for every admitted Vermont student from a household earning up to $125,000 a year, up from $100,000 in 2025 and $60,000 when it started in fall 2023. UVM says that means most Vermont families can now send a student to the flagship with tuition and fees fully covered.

Colchester girl earns Scout's highest rank with Little Free Library project — Colchester High 10th grader Juliet Wager is the first girl in her troop to reach Eagle Scout, for building the Snack and Books pantry, a Little Free Library near Mallets Bay School stocked with canned goods and books. She says it gives people a second option if they feel awkward signing up for a food shelf.

Burlington City Arts Announces New Grants for Visual Arts — BCA's new Verge Artist Fund, backed by the Andy Warhol Foundation, will give out $60,000 a year in 10 to 15 grants of $2,500 to $7,000 for artist led projects outside traditional arts venues. Artists in Chittenden, Addison, Franklin and Grand Isle counties can apply starting Jan. 8, and the deadline is April 16.

Vermont seeks shelter for homeless residents with complex medical needs — The state wants to open at least one shelter for up to 25 homeless Vermonters with the most complex medical needs, run with a health care provider offering care like wound care and medication management, and hopes to have it running in the new year. It's part of reforms moving people out of motels and into shelters and treatment.

Burlington history museum seeks community support — The Burlington History and Culture Center is working toward a city history museum, backed by a Vermont Humanities grant of $10,000 a year for three years, and is still looking for a location and funding. Planned exhibits include the Black owned barbershops that lined Church Street and the Lebanese immigrant "Icemen," and organizer Elise Guyette wants your ideas (her email is in the story).

Burlyaxe Throwing closes Burlington space — Burlyaxe Throwing announced on Oct. 15 that it has closed its North Winooski Avenue location after seven years, a pandemic shutdown and, in its words, hundreds of thousands of axes thrown. Its mobile axe trailer is still available for event bookings.

Vermont replaces decades-old unemployment computer system — The Department of Labor launched a new online unemployment system, built with $30 million in federal relief money, to replace the 56 year old mainframe that crashed under the 2020 COVID layoffs. Labor Commissioner Kendal Smith says the system doesn't use AI to decide eligibility or appeals.

Firefighters to bring Christmas cheer at UVM children's hospital: where to donate — Burlington firefighters kicked off the 16th annual toy drive for patients at Golisano Children's Hospital. New toys and gift cards can go to drop off spots like Burlington Fire Stations 1 and 2, Burlington Police, Burlington Electric, and the fire departments in South Burlington, Winooski, Colchester and Essex Junction, and toys should be in by Dec. 16.

UVM launches ‘Vermont Listens’ civic engagement series — UVM's new Vermont Listens series, where neighbors hear from people with opposite points of view, starts tonight in Bennington. The Burlington session on Nov. 4 is for UVM community members, per UVM's event page.
