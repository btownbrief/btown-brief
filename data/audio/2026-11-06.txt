This is the Btown Brief for Friday, November 6th.

Soak up today, because it's the last 60 degree day for a while, with a few hours of sunshine and a high in the low to mid 60s. Saturday turns cooler and hangs around 50 with a north breeze gusting to 25 mph, and after a mostly dry morning, rain becomes likely from about 2. Rain is likeliest Saturday night into early Sunday, a quarter to a half inch that this dry fall could use, and Sunday's rain ends by early afternoon, leaving it gray and chilly in the 40s. For patio, swim, and sunset scores as the week goes on, keep Burlington Right Now handy.

[pause] In a ‘blue tsunami,’ how did Republican Gov. Phil Scott win another term?

"I just think he’s created a category of one, you know? And Democrats are willing to pull the lever for him even as they vote blue across the ballot," Baruth said, per VTDigger.

Scott beat Democrat Amanda Janoo 52.6% to 45.6% for a sixth two year term, something no other governor has done in the state's modern history, even as Democrats retook their legislative supermajorities and Molly Gray unseated his pick for lieutenant governor by 25 points. Janoo ran up her numbers in Burlington and Winooski but couldn't overcome Scott in ring towns like Shelburne, Williston and Colchester, and she drew about 57,000 fewer votes statewide than Rep. Becca Balint. Janoo outraised Scott over the final two months and topped $1 million overall, more than any Scott challenger since 2016, but the Republican Governors Association answered with $950,000 for a super PAC that questioned her experience. The seven point margin is still the narrowest of his runs for governor.

[pause] A Trolley Tour of Burlington Caters to the Deaf Community.

"Many people are afraid or don’t know how to interact with deaf people, so I’m hoping this will be an eye-opener and bring some light," Lipson said, per Seven Days.

UVM American Sign Language instructor Brittney Lipson, one of just four at the university, has teamed up with Burlington Trolley Tours for an hourlong ride around the city today, with operator Hannington Kasagga narrating while an ASL interpreter relays it. She hopes showing business owners that deaf people live and work here will nudge them to hire interpreters for events or have staff learn a few basic signs. Tickets are $35 and there's still room, so email bjalipson@gmail.com to grab a seat.

[pause] 16-year-old serves on Essex Junction City Planning Commission.

"I don’t think they actually realized my age because I never told them in the email. And they just said it was great to have a perspective from a kid my age," Finn said, per WCAX.

Finn Hamilton was 14 when he spotted a city email address on his parents' water bill and applied, and at 16 he sits on the Essex Junction City Planning Commission as a nonvoting member who brings neighbors' feedback to meetings. Housing is his focus, in the hope that new developments bring rents down by the time people his age are looking for a place, and he spends his free time at Burlington and South Burlington meetings scouting ideas to bring home. His advice for anyone curious is to reach out to your councilors, whatever your age.

[pause] B.U. Pub Gets New Owners — and a Remodel — in Colchester.

"(The regulars) do wellness checks; they bring in food; they pick up people who need rides," Rodney Oliveira said, per Seven Days.

The Porters Point Road dive in Colchester, a fixture since the 1970s, now belongs to Rodney, Tracie and Jake Oliveira, who bought it from Debra LeBlanc as she retires after 22 years of ownership. They've redone the bathrooms, floors and lighting, the original staff is staying, and an updated kitchen will slowly roll out homestyle dishes and bar food on weekends, with more games and maybe live music and trivia on the way. The grand reopening is Saturday.

[pause] Sarah George wins Chittenden County state’s attorney race.

"That means standing up for victims, holding people accountable and pursuing solutions that make our community safer," George said, per Vermont Public.

George, a Democrat, won another term as Chittenden County state's attorney with 65% of the vote against Republican Jace Laquerre, a Milton software engineer with no legal background who told voters he'd resign if elected so the governor could appoint a replacement. It caps a year in which she first had to survive a fierce Democratic primary, where her opponent argued the county had become less safe under her watch. Her new four year term starts in February.

[pause] John Killacky, Former Flynn Director and State Legislator, Dies at 74.

"Freedom of expression is a more precious commodity than taste," Killacky told a Brown University audience in 2016, per Seven Days.

Killacky, who died at home in South Burlington on Nov. 2, came to lead the Flynn in 2010 after making his name defending provocative art at Minneapolis' Walker Art Center during the 1990s culture wars. Over eight years he grew the Flynn's budget from $6 million to $7.7 million, nearly doubled its endowment, and opened the theater wider with discounted tickets through social service agencies and sensory friendly shows for kids. He then served two terms representing South Burlington in the House, where he was lead sponsor of the 2021 resolution apologizing for state sanctioned eugenics, and he kept making art to the end, showing "Metastasis," a piece about his cancer, at the South End Art Hop in September. May he rest in peace.

[pause] Democrats retake supermajorities in the Vermont Legislature.

"So it opens the window a little wider than it has been in the last session," Perchlik said, per Vermont Public.

Unofficial results grow House Democrats from 87 to 102 seats and Senate Democrats from 17 to 20, enough to override Scott's vetoes, while Republicans lose 18 House seats and Progressives more than double to seven. Appropriations chair Andrew Perchlik says that opens the door to ideas like a surcharge on income over $500,000 a year, though House Majority Leader Lori Houghton, who's running for speaker, says she'd still rather work with the administration. Burlington Progressive Brian Cina warns against using the numbers to ram things through, and Montpelier Rep. Kate McCann, another speaker hopeful, is wary of overplaying the hand after the 2023-2024 session, when Scott castigated lawmakers for a new payroll tax passed despite his vetoes. Scott sets the tone with his inaugural address and budget in January.

[pause] Workers at Lake Champlain Chocolates push to unionize.

"We simply believe a union is not right for Lake Champlain Chocolates at this time," Lampman said, per VTDigger.

Production, packaging and shipping workers at the chocolate maker, founded in Burlington in 1983, vote Nov. 17 on joining the Teamsters, in a proposed unit of 34 at a company that employs 108 people in Vermont. Organizer Nic Smith, who works at the Williston factory, says an internal survey found pay for some long serving staff below MIT's cost of living benchmark, $27.50 an hour for a single adult in Chittenden County, and healthcare costs are a sore spot. President Eric Lampman says raises have outpaced inflation and revenue growth for nine years and that a union would change the company's culture.

[pause] Glazed Donuts’ New Essex Café to Replace Leo & Co.

"We fully understand that Leo & Co. has become a third space for the local community and don’t want to change that," Lee said, per Seven Days.

Leo & Co. in the Essex Experience closes Nov. 13, and Glazed Donuts, which has shared the space since August, hopes to open its Glazed Café there in early December with breakfast burritos, a doughnut sandwich, lunch sandwiches, coffee, smoothies and dirty sodas. Favorites like the vegan blueberry muffin stay, the plan adds a kids' play area and a secret weekend pizza menu, and doughnuts are online preorder only during the switch. Owner Kayla Silver has also listed her Salt & Bubbles wine bar for sale, and it closes after a final New Year's Eve celebration if she doesn't find a buyer or a new business for the space by then.

[pause] Quick hits.

Gray wins back Vermont lieutenant governor job — Democrat Molly Gray is back in the lieutenant governor's job she held from 2021 to 2023 after beating Republican incumbent John Rodgers, who conceded Tuesday night. She says her priorities include lowering health care and housing costs, with a universal primary care system on her list.

Vermonters vote to approve 2 constitutional amendments — Prop 3 enshrines the right to collective bargaining and rules out future right to work laws, and Prop 4 guarantees equal protection regardless of race, ethnicity, sex, religion, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression or national origin. Late Tuesday the AP had Prop 3 at about 74% support and Prop 4 at 79%.

Voters largely reject local option tax questions in 3 towns — Essex approved a new 1% tax on room receipts but rejected local taxes on meals and retail sales, while Hinesburg and Bennington turned down every option. Because of an error in how the vote was warned, Essex residents will have to approve the rooms tax again at town meeting.

Church Street Marketplace still searching for 2026 Holiday Tree — The Marketplace has looked at about 20 trees and still needs this year's top block tree, a 30 to 35 foot blue spruce from Burlington, Williston, Winooski, Colchester or Essex Junction. Got one in the yard? Email churchstreetmarketplace@gmail.com or call 802-923-9257.

Williston student accepts nomination to be Vermont’s next ‘Kid Governor’ — Williston Central School fifth grader Oli Agoos won her school's October primary and is on the statewide ballot to be Vermont's next Kid Governor, running to help stop climate change in her community. Fifth graders across Vermont vote this month.

Eight young artists bring Burlington's water systems to life through new mural — Eight young artists from Burlington City Arts' youth apprenticeship program spent four weeks with Juniper Creative Arts on "Our Water, Our Future," unveiled Wednesday at the Burlington Water Resources building. It traces how the city's pipes, pumps, sewers and storm drains connect to Lake Champlain, and it stays on display there as new public art.

Jeweler opens new craft shop in downtown Shelburne — Jennifer Kahn, who has made jewelry for 25 years, opened So Many Moons at 5224 Shelburne Road, selling her pieces alongside handmade work from 13 regional artists who rent space and keep 100% of their sales. Classes and events are planned for the new year.

Street cleaning underway in Burlington to reduce pollution and stormwater runoff — Operation Clean Sweep runs overnight through Nov. 13, and DPW director Chapin Spencer says it typically collects about 800 yards of leaves and organic matter, nearly 100 dump truck loads kept out of Lake Champlain. Fall leaf pickup starts Nov. 16.
