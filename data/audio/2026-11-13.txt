This is the Btown Brief for Friday, November 13th.

Today stays dry under a stubborn gray sky, with a light north wind and a high near 41. Saturday tops out in the low 40s, and rain and snow showers fill in around midday and keep at it into the evening. Saturday night the showers turn to snow before dawn as temps settle near 30, though little or nothing should stick in town, while WCAX has Mount Mansfield picking up 4 to 6 inches over the weekend. [pause] Diversity of Old North End highlighted in new mural.

"It makes me feel happy to see my friends and I saw myself too," said Jeanneta, a fourth grader at IAA, per NBC5.

A 28 foot mural of a community feast is now hanging inside Integrated Arts Academy, built around the foods students told the artist matter to them, with some of their own artwork worked in. Artist Jozie Furchgott Sourdiffe wanted it to celebrate the diversity of the Old North End, and the students painted into the scene didn't know they were in it until the tarps came off. The whole thing took about 18 months.

[pause] Burlington City Council confirms Geraci as new police chief with 10-2 vote.

"At this moment, I am choosing Burlington, and up to this point, Burlington is choosing me," Geraci said, per VTDigger.

Anthony Geraci, 49, started as Burlington's police chief Tuesday after the City Council confirmed him 10-2 Monday night, with Democratic councilors Becca Brown McKnight and Evan Litwin voting no. Brown McKnight pointed to workplace discrimination allegations from his time as chief in Newburgh, New York, which News 12 reported an independent probe found unfounded, saying no one in the city had been able to review the underlying details, while Litwin faulted a long hiring process he said felt secretive. Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak said she vetted him personally and that the International Association of Chiefs of Police background check found no wrongdoing. Geraci has bought a house in Burlington, will earn about $185,000 a year, and named staffing, recruitment and retention as the department's immediate challenges.

[pause] Last Call at Vermont Pub & Brewery in Burlington.

"VPB was the first brewpub in the state and one of only 11 in the country," per Seven Days.

Saturday is the last day for the College Street brewpub Greg and Nancy Noonan opened in 1988, and owners Elise Pecor and Scott Gawitt say business never recovered from its 2020 drop. The pub isn't taking reservations Saturday, so walk in and grab a drink at the bar while you wait for a table, though the Burly Irish Ale, on tap all 38 years, was the first beer to sell out. The brewing equipment goes to auction at the end of November, and the Alchemist is giving the two bronze patio lions and the crow topped sidewalk sign a new home.

[pause] Hong's Dumplings seeks new owner, closing in May.

"It’s time. Oh my god, life is so short," Hong said, per NBC5.

Hong Yu, 68, has served her family's dumpling recipe for nearly 27 years, first from a cart on Church Street and then from her Pearl Street storefront, and now she's ready to retire. The shop has been closed since mid-October but still fills orders on Fridays and Saturdays, and with her lease ending in May she's looking for a new owner, though she says most potential buyers so far aren't interested in keeping the dumpling business going.

[pause] Homelessness Rises in Chittenden County, Drops Across Vermont.

"With Chittenden County bearing the brunt of the state’s homelessness crisis, Burlington is engaged in a heated debate over how best to respond and whether or not the state should do more to assist the city," per Seven Days.

January's Point-in-Time Count found 3,286 homeless Vermonters, exactly 100 fewer than in 2025, but Chittenden County grew 1% to 834 people, well ahead of Rutland County's 434. The number of unhoused children rose 5% and now makes up about 20% of the homeless population, and more than two-thirds of people in emergency shelter got there through the motel program. Advocates say the count misses people staying with friends or family or paying for their own motel rooms, and heading into winter they're pushing for technical fixes to the emergency housing law passed in June.

[pause] Empty offices linger in downtown Burlington as work habits change.

"What concerns me about the vacant office space the most is that those office workers were built-in consumers for us, as well as those who visited their office," said Kara Alnasrawi of the city's Community & Economic Development Office, per WCAX.

Office vacancy in Burlington's central business district sat at 11.3% in June, up from 6.9% in December 2019, while the rest of the city and Chittenden County hit 11.9%. At Main Street Landing on the waterfront, the space that once housed Seventh Generation and its 100 employees is still empty. The city is counting on hundreds of new residential units to bring back that built in customer base, since turning offices into housing has worked in spots like Pine Street but is often too expensive.

[pause] Soundbites: Burlington Has Saved Rock and Roll.

"Our sound is weird, sometimes heavy, chock-full of post-punk aggression and balanced with evocative lyricism," per Seven Days.

Chris Farnsworth's column argues that this year's records from Greg Freeman, Robber Robber, Dari Bay and Lily Seabird add up to a Burlington sound the wider music world has noticed, from a scene that grew out of basement and DIY shows after the pandemic and came together around Radio Bean. Freeman has since moved to Chicago, and Farnsworth worries the current venue crisis could push other local bands to follow him out. Freeman's tour with Robber Robber, whose core duo Nina Cates and Zack James started out in a Brattleboro high school band, comes home to Higher Ground in South Burlington on Saturday, Nov. 21, a show he calls one of the most important for local music in a generation. Seabird plays Foam Brewers in Burlington on Dec. 12.

[pause] Chittenden parents file lawsuit challenging Vermont's new public tuition rules.

"What is happening here is people of faith are essentially being treated as second-class citizens," said Jacob Reed of Alliance Defending Freedom, per VTDigger.

Carrie and Greg Bishop are suing the Agency of Education in federal court, arguing that Act 73, the 2025 education reform law, created "a de facto religious exclusion" by leaving just 18 private schools in the public tuition program, none of them religious. Their district, Barstow Unified Union, only has public schools through eighth grade, and they want it to pay for their daughter's senior year at Mount St. Joseph Academy, a Catholic school in Rutland that doesn't qualify because its district has a public high school. Court documents from an earlier lawsuit show the law also cut 18 secular schools alongside 15 Catholic and Christian ones.

Vermont to raise minimum wage by 49 cents in January — The minimum wage goes from $14.42 to $14.91 an hour on Jan. 1, about $1,000 more a year for someone working 40 hours a week, and the tipped wage rises from $7.21 to $7.46. The raise is automatic under state law, which ties it to inflation, and this year's 3.4% increase set the size.

"Champ" on a stamp: Lake creature featured by USPS in 2027 — Lake Champlain's very own Champ, in turquoise, is one of 5 mythical creatures on a series of USPS forever stamps coming in 2027. The Postal Service hasn't said yet when or where they'll be available.
