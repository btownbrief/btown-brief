This is the Btown Brief for Monday, October 26th.

Keep a warm layer within reach this week, because the temperatures ride a roller coaster on the way to Halloween. Monday starts with some sun before clouds roll in ahead of a cold front, with a high near 57 and maybe a few sprinkles up north, though Burlington should stay mostly dry. Tuesday is sunny but sharply colder, topping out near 47 with a light north breeze, and Tuesday night drops to about 25, cold enough that a few spots around Vermont could see record lows Wednesday morning. Wednesday warms back up to a sunny 54, and Thursday is the pick of the week, mostly sunny and breezy from the south with a high near 63, before a chance of light showers Thursday night. Early looks at Halloween are dry and crisp, so plan on a jacket for trick or treating. Track it all week on my new weather page at Burlington Right Now.

Btown Brief Presents: Homeward Bound By Kwame Dankwa

BURLINGTON, Vt. — Siobhan Neela-Stock, a freelance journalist, has spent her career telling other people's stories. Now she's about to tell her own; tracing her roots back to India, where she was adopted as a baby, and preparing for a search that will finally tell her who she came from. In Kwame's 7 minute interview, Siobhan talks about being adopted by a white couple and growing up with three sisters also adopted from India, sometimes the only brown kids in their school, and about the year she finally went through her adoption papers and came away with more questions than answers, starting with her own birthday. She has since written about South Asian adoption for The Juggernaut and learned from other Indian adoptees that what the records say is not necessarily true.

Siobhan was born in India and adopted at a year and a half, and she grew up in Vermont. To find her birth family she has enlisted Arun Dohle and Anjali Pawar of the Adoptee Rights Council, which helps Indian adoptees find their biological families, and her GoFundMe covers the first three years of search costs, with more than $3,100 raised toward its $11,268 goal so far, so give if you can, and share her story with anyone it might reach.

[pause] Essex Experience Goes Up for Sale, but Longtime Owner Peter Edelmann Plans to Stay Involved.

"I've been a shepherd long enough," per Seven Days.

Edelmann, 73, who turned a dying outlet mall into a hub of locally owned shops, cafés and makers, has listed the shopping center for $27.75 million, but he'll keep Essex Cinemas, which he plans to upgrade, and the Double E performance center. He's also building Phun, a 15,000 square foot entertainment center next to the Hannaford with duckpin bowling, high tech darts, an arcade and a sports bar. He says he wants a buyer who'll hold true to what they've built and won't price the tenants out.

[pause] Desjardin inspires Seahorses soccer team.

"And what this young lady has gone through, our team just rallied around her," per WCAX.

Mariella Desjardin is one of just a couple of freshmen on Burlington High School's varsity girls soccer team, and she's playing through kidney disease that has now put her on the deceased donor list for a transplant, so the call could come at any time. Before their game against Essex the Seahorses held a special ceremony for their teammate, encouraging people to sign up as organ donors with Donate Life America or even explore living kidney donation. She says she's looking forward to staying on varsity and playing for the next three years.

[pause] Vermont experts say bats need our help. How do we get over our fear of them?

"People who were like, 'Bats are gross and scary,' said, 'I still think they're gross and scary, but I know they're in trouble, and I don't want to hurt them,'" per VTDigger.

It's International Bat Week, a good excuse to get to know the nine bat species that call Vermont home, five of which are listed as threatened or endangered, and white nose syndrome has killed nearly 6 million bats across the Northeast since 2006. Milton's Barry Genzlinger, known as Barry the Bat Guy, estimates he has rehabilitated at least 1,000 bats and keeps the ones people find in their homes over winter safe until he can release them in spring. If you find a bat inside in the middle of winter, don't toss it out in the snow, call the Vermont Bat Center instead. This is also the time of year bats look for a place to hibernate, so Fish & Wildlife has guidance for safely keeping them out of your attic.

[pause] 'Vermont is not a climate refuge': UVM researchers release new climate assessment.

"Our aging infrastructure, aging population and rural mountainous geography make us more vulnerable to climate impacts," per Vermont Public.

UVM's third Vermont Climate Assessment, released Friday, finds the state warming faster than the global average, with Burlington now seeing triple the days above 90 degrees it did in 2020 while just a third of Vermont households have air conditioning. The surface of Lake Champlain is on average 7 degrees warmer than in the 1960s, fueling the cyanobacteria blooms that regularly close the only beaches Burlingtonians can walk to. One bright spot is skiing, which the last assessment questioned by century's end, since researchers now expect ski areas to have the water they need for snowmaking.

[pause] Bernie Sanders Hits the Campaign Trail With Amanda Janoo in Winooski.

"This is about making health care free at the point of care, while building a publicly funded system that is accountable to the people it serves," per Seven Days.

Sanders joined the Democratic/Progressive candidate for governor outside the Community Health Centers in Winooski, his first time campaigning publicly alongside her since endorsing her on Oct. 7, to champion her plan for publicly funded primary and preventive care. Janoo estimates the program would cost at least $320 million a year and says higher taxes on wealthy Vermonters could raise roughly $260 million of it, while Gov. Phil Scott argued in their debate that Vermont can't afford another experiment like that and it has to be done nationally.

[pause] Vermont weighs new tax on high earners amid budget pressures.

"What we have is folks who are running on national political rhetoric promising to tax oligarchs, when all we have to tax is oncologists," per WCAX.

The Fair Share campaign, backed by Janoo and Molly Gray, claims raising taxes on households making $250,000 or more could bring in $345 million a year for primary care, with an extra 2% on some 22,000 households under half a million and an extra 8% on about 4,500 households above it. The Lake Champlain Chamber of Commerce says those households already pay more than a third of Vermont's income tax and worries they'll leave, while the Public Assets Institute's Anika Heilweil says many higher earners are older and not as mobile. Scott, if reelected, would almost certainly veto it.

[pause] Vermont commits $15.3M to expand heating assistance amid high fuel prices.

"Any additional funds that can help prevent a family from running out of home heating fuel, from having their pipes burst, is money well spent," per VTDigger.

Following up on Friday's draft plan story, the state's Emergency Board unanimously approved the package Friday, with heating oil up about 76% since last fall and kerosene up 61%. It raises this year's seasonal aid for households heating with oil and kerosene, likely covering about 62% of their fuel needs instead of the usual 45%, and doubles the crisis fuel program so an extra 2,400 households can avoid a heating emergency. Benefits generally go out in November.

[pause] A Vermont woman scrubs grime from gravestones and researches who is buried there.

"It's like journalism, but for dead people," per the AP via Vermont Public.

Caitlin Abrams is one of the many creators posting videos of herself cleaning graves, focusing on 18th and 19th century headstones in remote Vermont graveyards like a modest one in Rupert, and researching the young women and children buried there through birth, marriage and death records and old newspaper clippings. A restoration professional warns that aggressive brushing can damage old stones, and Abrams agrees that proper cleaning and permission from the cemetery come first. She hopes her work helps take the fear out of cemeteries.

[pause] AI Political Ads Are Proliferating in Vermont, but Are Clearly Fakes.

"AI, like many technologies, is a tool and, like any tool, can be used to harm people or help them," per Seven Days.

AI political ads have arrived in Vermont, mostly as campy parodies instead of the deceptive deepfakes lawmakers moved to curb this year, like Rep. Becca Balint cast as Dr. Evil and Molly Gray as Mini-Me. A federal judge blocked the attorney general's probe of one AI video of Balint, ruling it would likely qualify for the law's satire exemption. Sen. Ruth Hardy, the lead sponsor of the state's AI law and a frequent target of retired history teacher Curtis Hier's Flip Vermont Red memes, sees a pattern of conservative men going after Democratic women, though she says the attacks have mostly rallied supporters to her side.

[pause] Officials advance plan for parks and open space in South Burlington.

"This will not deliver an answer, but what it will do is deliver the policy deliberation that allows you to articulate how and where investments are being made based on a framework," per The Other Paper.

South Burlington meets all but one of its own park standards, and the gap is a park or nature area within walking distance of every resident, with the areas around Route 7, East Terrace and the streets near Rice Memorial High School among those without one. Only one of the city's 11 recreation parks has a permanent bathroom and two have drinking fountains, and contractor CivicSol suggests fixing basic amenities first at parks that serve the most people. The city is taking public comments on the draft plan through Oct. 31 and expects to finalize it in November.

[pause] Chris Taylor steps down as VGFC men's head coach.

"The impact of Chris Taylor on this football club is immeasurable," said Vermont Green FC co-founder Matthew Wolff, per WCAX.

Taylor is stepping down after two years in charge to spend more time with his family, leaving with a 45 game unbeaten run, a Steinbrecher Cup and back to back USL2 national championships, the first won on Maxi Kissel's 91st minute winner in front of a madhouse at Virtue Field. His teams won 38 games, drew 7 and lost zero, with seven trophies in total. The club has already started searching for his replacement.

[pause] Free haircuts in an art gallery? This installation is more than a snip off the top.

"I'm interested in the space of a barber shop being a vehicle for having conversations," per Vermont Public.

For one week this month, artist and scholar Ace Lehner turned a Burlington City Arts gallery on Church Street into a barbershop art project, with a giant disco ball, a pink and zebra striped rug and a comically large red comb, where Lehner, who is not a trained barber, gave free haircuts on a first come, first served basis. Before each cut, people committed to an act of "queer world building," like starting a weekly game night, and afterward each one wrote that commitment on a Polaroid that went up on the wall. The residency has since wrapped with a closing performance.

UVM trustees approve up to $10M for Tarrant Center — The long delayed multipurpose center between Gutterson Fieldhouse and Patrick Gym, with a new arena for both basketball teams and concerts, is moving again after trustees authorized up to $10 million for pre-construction, made possible by a $10 million appropriation from the Legislature. Next up is figuring out the final cost and how much more fundraising it needs.

He delivers them himself to parks, bike paths and the Burlington waterfront, and now Winooski and South Burlington too.

$60 million coming to Vermont for rail line completion — Vermont's congressional delegation announced $60.3 million in federal funding to complete about 60 miles of the western corridor rail line, which leaders say will connect western Vermont to New York's Penn Station.

El Gato Cantina Reopens in Essex Junction as El Bar — After its planned move into the old Silver Palace on Williston Road fell through over building problems, El Gato is back at 4 Park Street as El Bar, a sports cantina with a full bar, pool, darts, a simplified menu with new tamales and chiles rellenos, and comedy, open mics, DJs, drag and karaoke.

Rodgers, Gray face off in Vt. lieutenant governor WCAX debate — Both candidates already know the job, with Republican John Rodgers first elected in 2024 and Democrat Molly Gray holding the seat from 2021 to 2023. Gray is running on lowering health care and housing costs with universal primary care, and Rodgers on helping small businesses grow and reforming Act 250.

Blue Paddle Bistro files for bankruptcy — The beloved South Hero restaurant, which closed in August after 21 years, filed for Chapter 7 on Sept. 29, reporting more than $27,000 in assets and nearly $589,000 in liabilities.

Winter outlook favors fewer cold days, more ice/mix events — NOAA's outlook favors a warmer than normal December through February in Vermont, meaning fewer prolonged cold stretches and subzero nights and more chances for rain, sleet or ice instead of snow. Burlington normally gets about 60 inches of snow over those three months.

Vermont's unemployment rate remains low, but its aging population is leaving the workforce — Vermont's jobless rate held at 2.6% in September, tied for third lowest in the country, and Chittenden County was lowest in the state at 2.2%. The labor force shrank by nearly 10,000 people in the past year as baby boomers retire.
