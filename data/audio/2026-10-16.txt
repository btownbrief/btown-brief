This is the Btown Brief for Friday, October 16th.

Find the warm coat, because this weekend swings from soggy to wintry to gorgeous in three days. Friday is wet and windy, with rain heavy at times, a break around mid morning, then more showers along a cold front this afternoon and early evening, a high near 60 and south gusts around 40 mph in the Champlain Valley that could bring down a few branches. The showers end this evening as the wind turns northwest, and tonight drops to the mid 30s while the mountains turn to snow. Saturday is the coldest day of the season so far, with highs only around 40, northwest gusts near 30 mph and morning wind chills in the 20s, and a few stray flakes could even mix in down here in the morning before the sun comes out in the afternoon. Saturday night drops right to freezing, then Sunday turns sunny with a high in the mid 50s, and Monday is sunny and near 60 to start a long dry stretch. Track it all week, with patio, swim, and sunset scores, at Burlington Right Now.

[pause] New plans could bring Burlington’s Nectar’s back to life.

"It’s very exciting news. You know, I’ve long thought that the closing of Nectar’s was one of the big reasons that this stretch of town was so barren for a while," per WCAX.

The Main Street club has been closed since last year, and Shawn Cliche has submitted a zoning application to use all three floors for food, live music, DJs and pool tables, day and night. He says it's still undecided whether the Nectar's name stays, and there's a long process ahead before music returns. Cliche has opened about 30 bars and clubs over the years, including a similar venue in Plattsburgh.

[pause] Yên Vietnamese Café & Kitchen to Launch Soon in Winooski.

"It means the sense of peace and comfort that you feel on the inside," per Seven Days.

Summer Cao, 27, moved Donwoori Korean restaurant to 22 Main Street by the Winooski rotary and kept the lease on its old home at 65 Winooski Falls Way, where she expects to open Yên by the end of October. The 35 seat restaurant honors her late father with pho, fresh summer rolls, sticky fish sauce chicken wings and Vietnamese coffee made with robusta beans, plus mocktails and cocktails, for lunch and dinner six days a week.

[pause] Burlington mayor signs executive order on ICE enforcement; DHS pushes back.

"This is nothing more than a political stunt by Vermont sanctuary politicians," per NBC5.

Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak's order, signed Wednesday, lays out how Burlington will carry out Act 150, the state law in effect since June that restricts civil arrests, including immigration arrests, at schools, churches, libraries, hospitals, polling places and other designated locations. The city will update its signs, train employees on responding to federal immigration enforcement and work with legal organizations to help residents understand their rights, and Burlington police will not assist with civil immigration enforcement. DHS says ICE isn't targeting hospitals, schools or churches and isn't planning operations at polling places.

[pause] ICE is more active in Vermont. But federal data overstates it.

"So ICE does appear to be on track to increase its interior arrests in Vermont this year, even if not as dramatically as the federal data suggests," per Vermont Public.

Widely cited federal data shows ICE logging 124 arrests in Vermont through July, more than triple all of 2025, but Vermont Public found more than 50 of those people closely match border crossers caught by Border Patrol and later handed to ICE, so their arrests were counted twice. ICE made 29 at large arrests in that stretch, including the March 11 detentions of three immigrants at their South Burlington home, compared with 35 in all of 2025. Since that data was compiled, Seven Days has identified at least 12 construction workers detained by ICE in August and early September.

[pause] ‘Fuck the Flock cameras’: On big tech, Vermont’s candidates for governor differ on style and substance.

"I think what you’re seeing here is an issue that really gets to the heart of the different governing philosophies between the two," per VTDigger.

Democratic nominee Amanda Janoo wants to ban Flock Safety's license plate reading cameras, which some Vermont police departments use, and to tell large data center companies they aren't welcome here for now, after Gov. Phil Scott vetoed a bill this year that would have regulated where they could be built. Scott says the federal government, working with the private sector, should take the lead on regulating AI. His campaign says he would support a moratorium on large data center construction while the state watches what Washington does, and Janoo backs one too, along with new taxes on digital advertising revenue and what her campaign calls the nation's strongest public standards for AI. Polls and campaign spending suggest this could be the closest governor's race in a decade.

[pause] Pro-Phil Scott super PAC launches ad blitz targeting Amanda Janoo.

"National Republicans don’t spend almost half a million dollars to help keep a governor in office without a reason," per Vermont Public.

A Stronger Vermont, the Republican Governors Association's super PAC that spent $3 million to help elect Scott in 2016, spent nearly $175,000 late last week on TV and online ads painting Janoo as too far left, using a "magical thinking" line from her primary opponent Aly Richards, who has since criticized the RGA for misappropriating her quotes. Scott's campaign says the ads make fair points and that the RGA's help won't change his approach to the Trump administration. The latest Vermont Public poll put Scott's approval at 45%, down 15 points from 2022, and Janoo has outraised him $706,000 to $414,000.

[pause] Kismayo Kitchen Reopens in BTV on Riverside Ave — for Now.

"Mohamed and Abdirisak both emphasized that they are actively seeking a new Chittenden County home for Kismayo Kitchen," per Seven Days.

The Somali American restaurant founded by the late Ahmed Omar reopened the weekend of Oct. 10 at 505 Riverside Ave., now run by his widow, Anisa Mohamed, with siblings Maryan and Abdirisak Maalin, keeping the mix of samosas, goat curry and Philly cheesesteaks with a slight price increase planned. The building qualified for a FEMA buyout and will eventually be torn down for green space, most likely in 2027, so they're hunting for a spot with at least 24 seats. It's open noon to 8 Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday, and 2:30 to 9:30 on Fridays.

[pause] Sober living home to open in Burlington to give men 'fresh start' at life.

"This has saved my life on numerous occasions, and I'm hoping that even if I save one person's life, it means everything to me," per NBC5.

Fresh Start Recovery's Cedar Street house, also called Michael's Place in memory of Michael Prescott, who ran similar homes in Vermont and New York before his death in 2023, plans to open by the end of October. Eight men can live there at once for up to a year, five are already waiting to move in, and residents will get help finding jobs. The nonprofit still needs donations, from dishes to beds, and hopes to build a home for women next.

[pause] Previewing the Vermont International Film Festival in 48 Loglines.

"Last year, attendance at the annual Vermont International Film Festival hit a 13-year high, according to executive director Steve MacQueen," per Seven Days.

The 10 day festival opens today at Main Street Landing Performing Arts Center and runs through Oct. 25, with 48 features, several shorts programs and six classics from 1976. Seven Days sums up every feature in a line, from Palme d'Or winner Fjord and Hirokazu Kore-eda's Look Back to The Last Critic, a Robert Christgau documentary featuring Winooski's Autumn Records. Tickets run $6 to $12, or $150 for a festival pass.

[pause] New café offers space for both caregivers and kids to play.

"I’m just really excited to see kids in here using it and having fun and that’ll just make all my dreams come true right there," per Shelburne News.

Rachel and Andy Cartwright open Natural Nest Play Café at 2800 Shelburne Road on Monday, a Montessori style play space with toys made of natural, nontoxic materials for kids up to age 8, where caregivers supervise while they chat, drink coffee or get work done. Rachel says she's the first franchisee of the Delaware based business outside Delaware, and the building also has coworking space, private party rooms and a retail section. It holds 22 kids and their caregivers for now, with room to grow once Shelburne finishes its wastewater upgrade.

[pause] Essex Community Players Rebound From ‘Rocky Horror’ Drama.

"Roya and I were both saying we feel like we’ve come home," per Seven Days.

The all volunteer company canceled The Rocky Horror Show this spring after crew members received anonymous threats, a $5,800 hit that came as four of its nine board members resigned, and in August Essex Police said the threats came from the show's own music director. New board president Beth Wilbur sees a bright future, and the board says it will read licensing contracts carefully and monitor its shows more closely, though some former members, including 15 year veteran Thalie Stapleton, aren't convinced. The season opens tonight at Memorial Hall in Essex with Crime and Punishment, A Comedy, five actors playing 50 characters through Oct. 31 ($21 to $25).

[pause] Quick hits.

Offer extended to finalist in Burlington police chief search — Burlington has made an offer to a finalist for permanent police chief, pending a background check and the state's required polygraph, and Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak is aiming for City Council confirmation at the Nov. 9 meeting. Interim Chief Shawn Burke has led the department since former Chief Jon Murad retired.

Despite federal drama, mail-in voting proceeding as planned — Vermonters are receiving and returning mail-in ballots after the Supreme Court blocked President Trump's executive order on Sept. 14 and declined to let the Postal Service rule take effect for this year's election. State elections director Sean Sheehan says anyone who hasn't received a ballot by mid-October should check the Vermont Voter Portal or contact their clerk.

Bystanders rescue driver after camper van rolls into Lake Champlain — Bystanders jumped into Lake Champlain Monday morning to help a Quebec man out of his camper van after it slid backward down the boat launch at the Coast Guard station, apparently pulled in by the weight of a boat being loaded or unloaded. Burlington Fire and the Coast Guard pulled the van out of the water.

New life jacket law arrives just as the cold-water season starts — A new state law requires anyone on the open deck of a boat underway on Vermont waters to wear a Coast Guard approved life jacket from Nov. 1 through May 1 each year. It covers kayaks, canoes and paddleboards along with motorboats, sailboats and pontoons, and exempts Coast Guard inspected commercial vessels.

12-year-old rescued after becoming separated from family on Camel’s Hump — A 12 year old from Boston who got separated from her family hiking Camel's Hump last Saturday evening was found unhurt after about an hour, with 17 rescuers responding to the Waterbury Backcountry Rescue Team's search. Crews say to pack a flashlight or headlamp for hiking in the dark and stay with your group.
