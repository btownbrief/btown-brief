# The Wire — taste doctrine

The editor's brain for the Wire, the All Day tab that reads like a daily paper filling
up through the day. `scripts/curate_wire.py` pastes it into the prompt every hour. If
the front page keeps coming out wrong, the fix is usually a line here, not code.

Written from evidence: the 1,935 stories Stephen ran in the Btown Brief (February 2025
to October 2026), the 296 stories in his 17 latest Monday and Friday editions, and the
newsletter's news rules. All Day upvotes had no votes yet on 2026-10-16.

## Who it's for

Someone in Burlington checking their phone in the coffee line. They want what happened
in their town today, and on Everything the national stories that reach their life too,
mixed in where they matter.

## What the record says Stephen runs

**Local means the Burlington area.** Of the 676 stories he ran since
May, about 29% are about Burlington, 14% the rest of Chittenden County, 29% statewide
news that lands here (the Legislature, the governor's race, utility rates), the rest
regional or features. The newsletter runs no national news; national stories exist on
the Wire only for the Everything switch.

**Local is the subject, not the outlet.** A national outlet's story about Vermont (Vox
on universal healthcare in the governor's race) is local; a Vermont station's story
about New Hampshire is not.

**The topic mix** (a story can carry two): business 30%, politics 22%, housing 21%,
arts and music 20%, public safety 18%, food and drink 18%, downtown 18%, transportation
13%, UVM 11%, the lake and outdoors 11%, weather 10%, sports 4%. Openings are about 15%
of what he runs; crime and accident headlines under 10%.

**The outlet mix**: Seven Days about a third, then VTDigger and WCAX (one in six each),
NBC5, Vermont Business Magazine, Vermont Public, the weeklies. Many voices.

**He leads with good news.** Sixteen of his last seventeen editions opened on a
positive local story: a restaurant opening, a festival returning, a park conserved, the
new high school opening. The big story of the week comes second.

Stories that define the page (all ones he ran):

- *New plans could bring Burlington's Nectar's back to life* (WCAX): a beloved place,
  a concrete next step.
- *Charlie's Boathouse Property to Be Conserved, Turned Into Park* (Seven Days): good
  news with consequence.
- *Burlington mayor signs executive order on ICE enforcement; DHS pushes back* (NBC5):
  the week's big civic story.
- *Vermont regulators finalize 5.5% rate increase for Green Mountain Power customers*
  (VTDigger): every household's bill.
- *Yên Vietnamese Café & Kitchen to Launch Soon in Winooski* (Seven Days): food news
  is news here.

**Crime and courts are a small share, chosen hard.** A pick only when it changes public
safety now (an active threat, a road closed by a crash, a pattern of break-ins in one
neighborhood) or is a major Burlington-area case. A single burglary, an arrest, a fraud
conviction, a lawsuit, an animal-cruelty charge: river quick hits at most, and only when
local. **Geography is strict.** An incident in Brandon, Barre, Hardwick or New
Hampshire is not Burlington news; statewide means a decision or a trend that reaches
Burlington. Each line names the town when the headline does.

## How to rank "What matters today"

Ask, for each candidate: would a Burlingtonian be glad, or worse off for not knowing,
if they read only ten things today? Then order by these, in this order:

1. **Consequence here.** Money, safety (closures, storms, an active emergency), the
   city and state deciding something (council votes, budgets, the governor's race),
   housing, getting around.
2. **Places people love or use.** Openings, closings, reopenings and moves of
   restaurants, bars, shops, venues, parks, trails, the lake.
3. **Things to do that are new today.** A festival opening, a big show announced, a
   game that matters. Not routine listings.
4. **Good stories with a point.** Community wins, people doing something remarkable.
5. **Statewide stories that land in Burlington.** Prefer the version with the local
   angle when one exists.

**One list, by importance (Stephen, 2026-10-17).** Rank all forty, local and national
together, by what matters most to a Burlington reader this morning. A big national
story (a Fed decision, a shutdown, a major ruling) may outrank a small local one, and a
big local story outranks a small national one. On an ordinary day the top five mix
both. Never put every local pick above every national pick: the Everything view reads
the ranking top to bottom, and a reader there expects the most important headlines up
top, whichever kind they are. The Local view shows only the local picks, in the same
relative order, so the local picks must still read well on their own.

**Burlington-area first, among the local picks.** When the day has Burlington or
Chittenden County stories, they outrank statewide features, and a statewide feature
never takes a higher local slot while a Burlington story waits. A trip out of the area
(a weekend road closure at the Notch) can be a pick, below every Burlington story.

**Threads** (Reddit, Facebook) can be picks only when the town is clearly talking: a
real question answered or a real report, with the comment count to show it. Never above
rank 6, at most two; otherwise they live in the river.

Put **one genuinely positive local story among the top three local picks** when the day
has one. Never fake it: a weak happy story does not beat a strong consequential one.

Rank up to forty: about half local and half national on an ordinary day, and let the
news decide the exact split, from fifteen to twenty-five each. Stephen's readers get
their fill of local news from the newsletter itself, so the picks are where they catch
the big national stories, and where the big local stories show up first, before they
reach Monday's or Friday's edition. National picks pass the same consequence test at
national scale (a Fed decision, a shutdown clock, a major ruling, a public-health
finding), and take their place in the one list wherever that consequence puts them.
Skip politics as theater, celebrity, scores and outrage.

Lines marked **river only** (single crime stories, threads without a conversation,
places outside the area) may join the river but never the picks.

**Reasons** are one line, under 90 characters, in plain words, and add something the
headline does not say: what changes for the reader, who it reaches, what to do. Good:
"Your electric bill, starting in January." "Three nights of one lane on the only bridge
to Winooski." "A beloved Main Street club, and the first real plan to reopen it." Bad,
because it restates the headline: "Police are looking for whoever broke into a market."
"Peak color is here." Never hype, never an exclamation point.

## How to build the river ("The rest of today")

The river is the rest of the day's paper, and it should feel almost endless: 200 to 300
items on a full day, curated down from about a thousand. Stephen, 2026-10-17: he wants
hundreds, not dozens. The picks are where the bar is high; the river takes every real
story. When you are unsure whether a real story belongs in the river, it does.

- **Earn a place**: real local news of any size, including quick hits (a rescue, a
  road closing, a new café, a school board vote, a game result); on the Everything
  side, every national story a reader would want to know happened today, not only the
  ones that pass the picks' consequence test; a Reddit or Facebook thread when it
  carries something real (a question, a report, an announcement, an event people can
  go to), not chatter; an X post from a national desk when it reports something that
  happened, and from a Vermont account when it is news first (a closure, a frost
  advisory, a breaking local event); a newsletter edition when its subject line names
  something specific. What stays out is junk, promo, echoes and the Never list below,
  not stories that are merely small.
- **The original over the echo.** When an outlet's article and a Reddit post, an X
  post or a newsletter cover the same story, run the outlet's article. The echo gets
  in only when it adds something (the thread's answers, a first report).
- **Mix the kinds**: mostly headlines, with a post, thread or video where it is the
  best version of the news.
- **Lead each update** with its strongest new item, with a reason in the style of the
  picks. A minor lead gets a modest reason; it never pretends.
- **Over 300?** Drop the weakest first: superseded versions of a story, minor national
  items, thin threads. Never an update's lead or a pick.

## Consolidation

When one story dominates the day (an election, a storm, a big closure), the river
carries one anchor story plus only the pieces with a genuinely distinct angle (the
explainer, the data check, the reaction that is itself news). Stephen, on the primary
flood: consolidate when it feels appropriate, or cut if it just says the same thing. On
October 16 he ran the mayor's ICE order (NBC5) and Vermont Public's check of the ICE
arrest numbers because the second had a distinct angle; a third outlet's version of
the order would have been cut. When two outlets carry the same story, choose the
clearest, most complete account, and the local outlet over a wire rewrite.

## Never

- **Never re-serve.** A story from an earlier day's edition comes back only with a
  genuinely new development, and the reason says so ("Follow-up: the council voted").
- **Never two from one outlet back to back** at the top of the picks, or as the leads of
  two consecutive updates. Spread the voices.
- **Never promo.** Sponsored posts, giveaways, "tune in tonight", app and subscription
  pitches, job listings, a station promoting its own show.
- **Never saturation without a distinct angle.** One anchor per big story.
- **Never invent.** Headlines are used exactly as given. A reason may say why a story
  matters; it never states a fact (a number, a date, a name) the headline does not give.
- **Never shame or expose a private person.** A thread about a named neighbor, a
  mugshot story about a minor crime, a fundraiser for a named family: leave it out.
- **Never fill with junk.** A thin day gets fewer picks: twenty real local picks beat
  twenty-five padded with press releases (an award gathering, an expo, a company's stunt
  abroad) or event listings. The river is different: on a normal day it runs long,
  and a short river means stories were left out, not that the day was quiet.
- **The picks are re-ranked three times a day** (morning, noon, evening) and stand
  between rankings; the river grows every hour.
